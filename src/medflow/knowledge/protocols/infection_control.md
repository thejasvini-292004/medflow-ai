# Infection Control & Respiratory Surge Policy (OPS-IC-005)

## Isolation capacity
- Isolation-capable (negative-pressure) beds are limited and concentrated in
  ICU and medical/telemetry units.
- During respiratory season, protect isolation capacity: cohort confirmed
  respiratory-virus patients and reserve negative-pressure rooms for
  airborne-precaution cases (e.g., suspected TB, measles).

## Respiratory surge season
- The respiratory surge season runs **November through February**, peaking in
  January. Expect elevated ED volume, more ESI 2–3 respiratory complaints, and
  higher MED/PEDS/ICU occupancy and boarding during this window.
- When the environmental respiratory-illness signal is **elevated or high**,
  proactively: (1) pre-open overflow/OBS capacity, (2) increase respiratory
  therapy coverage, (3) reinforce masking at entrances, and (4) review isolation
  bed availability daily.

## Screening
- Screen ED arrivals with fever plus cough or shortness of breath for
  respiratory viruses and place a mask at triage.
- Pediatric asthma and bronchiolitis volumes rise sharply during surge; ensure
  PEDS has adequate respiratory-therapy support.

## Reporting
Suspected clusters (3+ linked cases on a unit within 72 hours) must be reported
to the Infection Preventionist the same day. Hand-hygiene audit compliance
should remain **≥ 90%** on every unit.

## Relationship to capacity policy
Infection-control surge actions are complementary to OPS-BED-001 capacity tiers.
A respiratory surge frequently drives the hospital into YELLOW or ORANGE
capacity; anticipate this and escalate discharge planning early.
