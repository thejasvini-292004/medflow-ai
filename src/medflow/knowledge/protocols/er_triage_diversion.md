# Emergency Department Triage & Diversion Policy (OPS-ED-002)

## Triage standard
The ED uses the 5-level **Emergency Severity Index (ESI)**:
- **ESI 1** — immediate, life-threatening (resuscitation).
- **ESI 2** — high risk / emergent; target door-to-provider < 10 minutes.
- **ESI 3** — urgent, multiple resources expected.
- **ESI 4** — less urgent, one resource.
- **ESI 5** — non-urgent, no resources.

## Door-to-provider targets
- ESI 1–2: median door-to-provider **≤ 10 minutes**.
- ESI 3: median **≤ 30 minutes**.
- ESI 4–5: median **≤ 45 minutes**.
A shift median above target for ESI 3 is an early crowding indicator and should
prompt a charge-nurse review.

## Left-without-being-seen (LWBS)
LWBS is a key crowding and access metric. Target LWBS rate is **< 2%** of
arrivals. An LWBS rate above 3% on any day requires a debrief documenting the
contributing factors (volume, boarding, staffing).

## ED diversion (ambulance bypass)
Diversion is a last resort and is reviewed — never triggered automatically.
Consider requesting diversion only when **all** of the following hold:
- the hospital is at **RED** capacity per OPS-BED-001,
- ED boarders ≥ 15 **and** occupied ED beds ≥ 90% of staffed ED beds,
- no ICU or telemetry beds are available for the next anticipated admission.

Diversion requires approval from the ED Medical Director and the
Administrator-on-Call, must be time-limited to 2 hours and re-evaluated, and
must be logged with start/stop times. Trauma, STEMI, and stroke receiving
status are **never** placed on diversion.

## Surge staffing trigger
When ED arrivals exceed 55 in a day or ESI 1–2 arrivals exceed 15, the charge
nurse should request an additional provider and consider opening the fast-track
area for ESI 4–5 patients to protect throughput.
