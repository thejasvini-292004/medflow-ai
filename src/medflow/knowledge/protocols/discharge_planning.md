# Discharge Planning & Readmission Reduction Policy (OPS-DC-004)

## Goal
Improve patient flow by discharging safely and early, and reduce avoidable
30-day readmissions, especially for heart failure (HF) and COPD, which are the
hospital's highest-readmission diagnosis groups.

## Early-discharge standard
- Target **40% of daily discharges completed before noon**. Morning discharges
  free beds when the ED admission demand peaks in the afternoon.
- Each inpatient unit identifies likely next-day discharges during the afternoon
  bed-huddle and writes conditional discharge orders where appropriate.
- The **discharge lounge** should be used for patients who are medically ready
  but awaiting rides or prescriptions, to release the physical bed sooner.

## Length of stay (LOS)
- Expected LOS varies by unit; ICU and medical/telemetry stays are longest.
- A stay exceeding **1.5× the unit's typical LOS** should trigger a case-manager
  review for barriers (placement, insurance authorization, consults).

## 30-day readmission reduction bundle
For HF and COPD discharges, complete the bundle before discharge:
1. medication reconciliation with the pharmacist,
2. a **follow-up appointment scheduled within 7 days**,
3. teach-back education on warning signs and daily weights (HF),
4. a 48–72 hour post-discharge phone call.
Readmission within 30 days for these groups is tracked monthly; a rate above
**20%** for either group triggers a root-cause review.

## Payer considerations
Discharge planning must account for payer authorization timelines. Self-pay and
Medicaid placements frequently take longer to arrange post-acute care, so begin
placement work early in the stay.
